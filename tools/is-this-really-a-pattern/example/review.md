# Review: Team Retrospective Pattern Review

I compared the saved [output](output.md) with the fictional [source log](log.md). This is a retrospective review of an illustration, not a logged model test or an independent check of the events.

## What Worked

It refused to merge on shared wording alone. Entries 1 and 2 both use the word "communication", which is the kind of surface likeness that gets lumped into one pattern. The output kept them apart once the recorded causes turned out to be different. It also named the surface pattern as misleading rather than quietly steering round it.

It grouped different wording behind the same recorded cause. Entry 1 says "Communication was frustrating this sprint" and entry 3 says "Felt like nobody knew what I was doing for days". The output grouped their blocked-review accounts together.

It called an isolated signal an isolated signal. Entry 2 (meeting load) and entry 4 (local environment) are both real, single instances. The output didn't inflate either into a pattern. It used the phrase "isolated signal" rather than making a one-off sound more established.

It gave no percentage from a four-entry sample. The output used counts and a plain confidence level (medium) for the one pattern, not a rate that a sample this size can't support.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

- Whether the suggested escalation rule is the right fix is for the team to decide. This review can't settle it.
- The output says three of four entries have a diagnosed cause, but all four rows contain a cause description. The log gives accounts of what happened, not separate evidence of how each diagnosis was established.
- The output infers a missing escalation process from two reviews that were not reassigned or escalated. That supports a recorded handling problem, not proof that no process exists. Its proposed process fix remains a suggestion.
- The quoted phrases for entries 1 and 3 are shortened extracts with changed initial capitalisation, not exact reproductions of the full source wording.
- The output shows no evidence against the pattern, which the skill asks for on every candidate. The two isolated signals get no low, medium or high confidence either.
- Count underlying events, not people. A repeat report about the same event adds an occurrence. A separate event may add an instance even if the same person reports it. Different people reporting one event do not necessarily create separate instances.

## Verdict

The main grouping matches the causes recorded in the fictional log. The count, process inference and omitted checks above still need correction. No automatic-failure rules or pre-run scoring criteria are recorded here, so I do not treat this as a formal pass.
