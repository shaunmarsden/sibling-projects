# Review: Support Ticket Log

I compared the saved [output](output.md) with the fictional [source log](log.md). This is a retrospective review of an illustration, not a logged model test or an independent check of the events.

## What Worked

It kept Dana's three export complaints together instead of treating them as three independent customers. The wording suggests an ongoing issue. Still, one customer does not automatically mean one underlying instance; a person should confirm whether these are repeat reports of the same unresolved event or separate failures.

It refused to invent a cause from wording alone. None of the five entries records a diagnosed cause. The output did not call the three topics confirmed same-cause patterns. That is supported; its claim that nobody did the diagnosis is not. The log shows what was recorded, not all the work that happened.

It declined the percentage request, as the skill instructs for a small or mixed sample. But its explanation goes too far. Three of the five logged tickets concern export: 60% is a valid description of these five ticket entries. It is not a rate across customers, a share of diagnosed causes or an estimate of wider demand. Repeat reports affect interpretation, not the arithmetic. The output should not imply that missing diagnoses make a descriptive ticket share invalid.

## What Still Needs a Human Check

- Check whether Dana's repeated ticket has investigation or diagnosis records outside this log. If not, that work is still needed.
- The output's final claim that the complaint was never investigated is unsupported. Ask for diagnosis records rather than assume no investigation happened.
- The output writes out "60%" as an example of what not to report. If that figure is used, label it as the share of these five logged ticket entries, not a wider rate or a share of customers.
- The output gives no evidence against any candidate, and no low, medium or high confidence, which the skill asks for on each one. It uses "isolated signal" instead.
- Once future tickets have diagnosed causes, it's worth reviewing this log again.

## Verdict

The output avoids claiming a diagnosed recurring cause, but its instance count needs confirmation. Its investigation claim is unsupported and its percentage explanation is too broad. No automatic-failure rules or pre-run scoring criteria are recorded here, so I do not treat this as a formal pass. The saved output is unchanged.
