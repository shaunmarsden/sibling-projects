# Do These Actually Match?

**One line:** Compare two independently-kept records of the same thing and surface only where they genuinely disagree, instead of a full side-by-side or picking whichever source seems more convenient.

**Generalises from:** the Conflicting evidence label named in [Practical AI Sales Workflows](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows)' [METHODOLOGY.md](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows/blob/main/METHODOLOGY.md) and given its own worked treatment in the [approval-gated sales copilot guide](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/practical-ai-sales-workflows/blob/main/guides/build-an-approval-gated-sales-copilot.md#label-the-evidence), stripped of anything sales-specific and pulled out as a standalone tool for the much more common case of two whole records that are supposed to match.

**Who it is for:** anyone reconciling two independently-kept records of the same thing, a manual count against a system export, one team's spreadsheet against another's, a membership list against who has actually paid, without wanting a wall of near-identical rows or a confident-sounding mismatch that turns out to be a unit conversion.

**Status:** Built, 22 August 2026. Live at [github.com/shaunmarsden/do-these-actually-match](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/do-these-actually-match), with a fictional hardware shop's stock count against its point-of-sale export testing a unit conversion, a formatting difference, a new delivery, and an incomplete count, and a second, harder fictional case testing a mismatched identifier that cannot be confirmed, a duplicated payment row, and a genuine status conflict.
