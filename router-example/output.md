# Routing Decisions: Two Cases

Source: [cases.md](cases.md), fictional.

## Case A

**Objective:** work out what to actually do about a friend who has not confirmed or declined joining the book club after six weeks.

**Recommended tool:** [What's Actually Causing This Delay?](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/whats-causing-this-delay)

**Why this route fits, not Post-Mortem Builder:** six weeks of silence feels stale, which could tempt a route straight to Post-Mortem Builder, treating this as something to write off as over. But nothing has actually closed here: no explicit no has been given, and the situation is still genuinely open. Post-Mortem Builder is for something that has already ended one way or another; this has not. The delay diagnosis fits because the actual question is what kind of "still thinking about it" this is, genuine indecision, an unstated concern, or a soft no not yet said outright, not whether to treat it as already over.

**What it will need from you:** the exchange so far, exactly as it happened, how long the delay has gone on, and how many times it has come up.

**What still requires a person:** the diagnosis only identifies what is likely happening; deciding what to actually say next, and saying it, stays with you.

## Case B

**Objective:** work out whether three similar-sounding review complaints about loading time represent a genuine, actionable pattern.

**Recommended tool:** [Is This Really a Pattern?](https://github.com/shaunmarsden/is-this-really-a-pattern)

**Why this route fits, not Is This Actually a Good Fit:** this could sound like a fit question at a glance, deciding whether something is worth acting on, but it is not weighing multiple aspects of one single decision. It is checking whether three separate instances (three different customers' reviews) share a genuine common cause, rather than only sounding similar. That is exactly what the pattern checker is for; the fit checker would be the wrong tool here since there is no single decision with multiple aspects being weighed.

**What it will need from you:** the three reviews' actual wording, whether anything is known about what specifically caused each one's slow loading experience, and whether these are genuinely three different customers rather than one person mentioned more than once.

**What still requires a person:** deciding whether a confirmed pattern is actually worth fixing, and any resulting product or process change, stays with you.
